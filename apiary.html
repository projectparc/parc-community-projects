PARC is helping the Chui Mama Women's Group turn an underperforming apiary into a women-led honey enterprise, relocating hives to three forage-rich satellite sites and training 20+ certified beekeepers. Metrohub has completed construction of the first apiary at the Chui Mama farm site, with Pankaj Foundation committed to purchasing 50% of the raw honey.

2. Purpose and objective

An existing 100-hive apiary had low colonisation due to limited forage, inconsistent water, limited beekeeping skills and low community ownership. PARC is rebuilding it as a women-led honey enterprise across the Chui Mama farm and satellite sites.

3. Scope

  • Included: Langstroth hives on elevated stands; predator-proof mesh enclosure with electric fence; 5,000 L rainwater tank with automatic float-valve watering trough; protective beekeeping gear; relocation of 75 hives to three satellite sites (25 each); training and mentoring (construction: Metrohub; beekeeping consultant: Bananah Samuel).
  • Excluded: Day-to-day hive management, which is the Chui Mama Women's Group's responsibility after training.

6. Community and partner commitments

The Chui Mama Women's Group manages the hives and honey production. Pankaj Foundation, with Loisaba Conservancy, supports the project and is the raw-honey offtake partner. Metrohub built the apiary and Bananah Samuel provides training and mentoring.

7. Operations and sustainability

The Chui Mama Women's Group runs the apiary, with monthly mentoring visits for six months and support from PARC's field team.

8. Main risks

  • Low hive colonisation — addressed through improved forage, a reliable water trough and training.
  • Predators and theft — mitigated through the mesh enclosure and electric fence.

9. Measures of success

  • Hives actively colonised
  • Women's Group managing hives independently
  • Honey harvested and sold through the offtake partner
