2. Purpose and objective

Esiteti relied on an unreliable, diesel-dependent borehole supply for domestic, school and livestock water. PARC solarised the borehole to give the Esiteti community, Esiteti Primary School and area livestock and wildlife reliable, low-cost access to water in the Amboseli ecosystem, offsetting an estimated 3,688 L of diesel a year.

3. Scope

  • Included: Supply and installation of a 8.2 kW solar PV array (15 x 550W Jinko panels) and Lorentz PSK3-7 (5.5 kVA) pump controller driving a 5 kW pump sized for a design abstraction of 35 m³/day; supply to Esiteti Primary School, the Esiteti community and a water trough for livestock and wildlife; testing and commissioning (contractor: Centre for Alternative Technologies).
  • Excluded: Routine day-to-day operation, which is the community's responsibility; scheduled maintenance is provided separately under the O&M arrangement with CAT.

6. Community and partner commitments

The Esiteti community provided site access and input on the supply points, and manages day-to-day use of the system. Elewana Collection partnered with PARC on the project. Centre for Alternative Technologies (CAT) delivered the solar pumping works and provides scheduled maintenance.

7. Operations and sustainability

The Esiteti community operates the system, with biannual scheduled maintenance by CAT and support from PARC's field team.

8. Main risks

  • Wear on storage and trough infrastructure (trough, school tank, tank roof) — addressed through scheduled maintenance and repairs coordinated by PARC with CAT.
  • Wildlife use of the water point damaging exposed infrastructure — managed through the dedicated livestock and wildlife trough and regular site checks.

9. Measures of success

  • System commissioned and handed over to the community
  • Reliable daily supply within the design capacity (35 m³/day)
  • Scheduled maintenance carried out and logged
  • Community and livestock water access maintained year-round
