2. Purpose and objective

The Lekasuyian Community Borehole needed a full turnkey solar-powered water supply system to reliably serve an estimated 1,200 people and 5,000 livestock, on a site prone to vandalism, theft and wildlife interference. PARC procured and delivered the borehole pump, elevated tank platform, livestock trough, tokenised distribution point and a human- and wildlife-proof security fence, restoring safe, self-sustaining water access for the community.

3. Scope

  • Included: Borehole servicing and aquifer testing by Geobrix (borehole flushing, camera inspection, test pumping) confirming a safe pumping yield of 6.4 m³/hr from the 100 m-deep borehole; supply and installation of a submersible solar pump and PV array sized to the 32 m³/day and 6 m³/hr abstraction caps, with dry-run/surge protection and cloud-connected remote monitoring; RC tank platform and clad solar shed housing a 10,000 L tank; a 12.0 m concrete livestock trough with tamper-proof valve chamber and automatic float valve; a tokenised water distribution point (3 outlets) integrated with the Maji Milele Water Token ATM; a 1.95 m combination chain-link/electric security fence around an approx. 8 m x 10 m platform with a 4 m vehicle gate; a weatherproof security camera; and full testing, commissioning and handover (contractor: Zoomspace & James Thuranira (fencing works)).
  • Excluded: Water Token ATM hardware and software, supplied and integrated directly by Maji Milele; routine post-handover operation and maintenance, which is the Lekasuyian Water Committee's responsibility.

6. Community and partner commitments

The Lekasuyian community/Water Committee provided site access and input on siting, and committed to token clerk appointment and ongoing Committee monitoring. Zoomspace delivered the civil, structural and electromechanical works to RFQ LEK 002 and the issued drawings. The electric security fence was subcontracted to James Thuranira. Maji Milele supplied and integrated the Water Token ATM and payment system.

7. Operations and sustainability

The Lekasuyian Water Committee owns and operates the system, with day-to-day maintenance supported by PARC's field team. Operating and minor repair costs are met from revenue collected through the Maji Milele token ATM. PARC provided post-handover monitoring and a joint defects inspection before Final Acceptance.

8. Main risks

  • Borehole casing encrustation and suspended particles noted at servicing — addressed through borehole flushing and extended test pumping before handover; continued pumping recommended to fully clear residual sediment.
  • Wildlife and vandalism damage to exposed infrastructure — mitigated through the 1.95 m human/wildlife-proof fence, welded steel pipe cladding, and a fully clad tank platform and solar shed.
  • Water Token ATM integration sequencing complicating commissioning — managed through direct PARC–Maji Milele coordination on training and token supply.
  • Livestock trough capacity insufficient for herd numbers at peak periods — assess actual peak herd counts against trough throughput and, if the shortfall is confirmed, plan a trough extension or a second trough with the Water Committee.

9. Measures of success

  • Final Acceptance Certificate issued for civil, solar and fencing works
  • System uptime within the 32 m³/day and 6 m³/hr abstraction caps
  • Water Token ATM revenue collected by the Water Committee
  • Community and livestock water access restored
