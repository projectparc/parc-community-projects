2. Purpose and objective

The borehole's ageing solar pumping system had degraded, giving insufficient, unreliable water for the Chui Mama farm's irrigation and the wider community's domestic and livestock use. PARC rehabilitated and re-solarised the borehole.

3. Scope

  • Included: Supply and installation of a Grundfos SP3A-25 submersible pump; 3,600 Wp solar array (18 x Dayliff 200W panels); Sunverter SV3 3.7 kW controller; Dayliff multijet water meter; new 32 mm HDPE distribution pipeline; electrified perimeter fence; testing and commissioning (contractor: Davis & Shirtliff).
  • Excluded: Routine day-to-day operation, handled by a community-based plumber through Loisaba Conservancy.

6. Community and partner commitments

Loisaba Conservancy co-implemented the project. Davis & Shirtliff delivered the works. The Chui Mama Women Group and Ewaso community use the water for the farm and domestic needs.

7. Operations and sustainability

A community-based plumber, engaged through Loisaba Conservancy, carries out operation and maintenance, supported by PARC's field team. Surplus water feeds the Ewaso community water tank.

8. Main risks

  • Farm irrigation demand exceeding supply — addressed through farm water-efficiency measures and metering.
  • Wildlife damage to equipment — mitigated through the electrified perimeter fence.

9. Measures of success

  • System commissioned and handed over to the community
  • Reliable daily supply within the design capacity (20 m³/day)
  • Scheduled maintenance carried out and logged
  • Community and livestock water access maintained year-round
